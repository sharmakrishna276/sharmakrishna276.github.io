# About Me

I'm interested in the parts of computing that most abstractions hide: the
instructions a processor executes, the bytes moving through memory, the
threads competing for resources, and the nanoseconds that separate a good
implementation from a great one.

I'm Krishna, a Maths and Computing major from IIT Delhi, currently exploring
the intersection of algorithms, systems, and hardware.

This portfolio is my engineering logbook — a place to document what I build,
what I learn, and how I push performance further. My interests lie in
low-latency systems, GPU programming, CUDA kernel optimization, concurrency,
and performance engineering.

I enjoy going beyond abstractions to understand what actually happens under
the hood: how CPUs execute instructions, how memory hierarchies shape
performance, how threads coordinate, and where hardware becomes the
bottleneck.

You'll find experiments, implementations, benchmarks, technical deep dives,
and projects exploring the boundaries between software and hardware.

I care about understanding *why* something is fast, not just making it work.
This is an evolving record of my curiosity, progress, and pursuit of building
systems that are efficient down to the last cycle.
